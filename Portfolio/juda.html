Bicycle Infrastructure & Safety

Analyzing 15,573 police-reported crashes to evaluate how infrastructure affects cyclist safety

Research Overview

This comprehensive study examined bicycle crash data across North Carolina to understand how different infrastructure types influence cyclist safety. Using spatial analysis and logistic regression models, we analyzed 15,573 police-reported crashes from 2007-2023 to identify patterns in injury severity and crash frequency.

Top 3 Crash Locations (In Charlotte)

  1. Tryon St & Trade St: 28 crashes (17% severe) – Mitigation: Protected bike lanes installed 2021 reduced crashes by 22%
  2. South Blvd & Remount Rd: 24 crashes (12% severe) – Mitigation: Added traffic calming measures in 2022
  3. Central Ave & Louise Ave: 21 crashes (9% severe) – Mitigation: Proposed bike signal in 2024 budget

Data source: NCDOT Crash Database 2007-2023

Sidewalks Safest

Crashes on sidewalks/crosswalks had the lowest probability of high-severity outcomes (2.5%) compared to travel lanes (7.6%).

Rural Hotspots

Rural counties like Robeson and Johnston emerged as hotspots for severe crashes, despite urban areas having more total crashes.

Environmental Factors

Fog increased severe crash odds by 2.5×, while unlit roadways at night doubled severe injury risk.

Road Classification

NC routes had 2.6× higher odds of severe crashes compared to local streets, one of the strongest predictors.

Key Insights

  • Dedicated bicycle infrastructure generally enhances safety, but effectiveness varies by context.
  • Sidewalks and crosswalks offer the most protection; bike lanes and paved shoulders provide moderate protection.
  • Multi-use paths showed the highest crash rate per mile (1.84), possibly due to data limitations.

Infrastructure Impact Scenarios

Current infrastructure shows a 7.6% severe crash rate in travel lanes and 2.5% on sidewalks.

If protected bike lanes were added to high-crash corridors, models predict a 22% reduction in severe crashes.

Without existing bike infrastructure, models estimate severe crashes would increase by 35% in urban areas.

Policy Recommendations

  • Prioritize sidewalk infrastructure in areas lacking dedicated bike facilities
  • Focus safety improvements on rural higher-order roads (US/NC routes)
  • Improve lighting along bicycle routes, especially in rural areas
  • Invest in better data collection, particularly for exposure and multi-use paths
  • Consider road surface quality in bicycle infrastructure planning

NIMBY Movements in Charlotte

Analyzing 307 public meetings to understand opposition to housing development

Research Overview

This study analyzed City Hall meetings in Charlotte to understand the messaging strategies of the NIMBY (Not In My Backyard) movement. Contrary to expectations, concerns about property values were less prominent than issues like traffic, density, and neighborhood character.

Key findings show that NIMBY opponents primarily framed arguments around neighborhood character rather than direct economic self-interest.

Top 3 Annexation Effects

  1. Service Expansion: Annexed areas gained city services (police, fire, water) within 2 years
  2. Tax Impact: Property taxes increased by average of 18% post-annexation
  3. Development: Commercial development increased 42% in first 5 years after annexation

Data source: Charlotte City Records 1855-2023

Dominant Concerns

"Traffic" was the most frequently used term (482 mentions), followed by "density" (187) and "character" (122).

Property Values Less Cited

Despite expectations, "property value" had only 25 mentions, the least of all target terms analyzed.

Neighborhood Character

85.2% of "character" mentions were tied to preserving neighborhood character against development.

Concentrated Opposition

Term usage peaked in 2022 despite fewer meetings, suggesting more intense opposition during that period.

Key Insights

  • NIMBY arguments focus on community impacts, not just financial self-interest.
  • "Character" and "density" are most associated with housing debates.
  • "Property value" is rarely cited directly, suggesting indirect framing of concerns.

Annexation Impact Scenarios

Current city boundaries provide services to 792,862 residents across 308.6 square miles.

Without annexation, Charlotte would only cover 30 square miles with 300,000 residents, leaving many areas without city services.

Policy Recommendations

  • Address traffic and density concerns directly in development proposals
  • Develop clear standards for preserving neighborhood character
  • Create mechanisms to validate or mitigate claimed impacts
  • Improve public engagement processes to surface real concerns earlier
  • Consider targeted communication strategies that address community preservation concerns
